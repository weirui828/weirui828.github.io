# Voice

Reference text for anything written on this site. Paste a passage from here as the sample
when using the humanizer skill, so the rewrite matches this voice instead of a generic one.

## The passage that reads final

Cave Diver, 03 The look:

> At first I wanted it in first person, full 3D, so you'd be inside the mask, looking down a
> tunnel with your own light. In a short jam that was too much to get right, so I went with
> something inspired by Little Nightmares instead. I love how that game is a full 3D world
> that you only ever see from the side, with stuff looming in the foreground and hiding in
> the back. Cave Diver works the same way: the cave is 3D, but the camera stays on the side.
> It turned out to fit caves really well. Real cave maps are drawn from the side, and from
> that angle you only ever see a thin slice of the cave, never what's behind the rock.
>
> Later I added depth. You can use R/F to swim into the background or back toward the camera,
> wherever you are, not just at marked holes. That one change made the cave feel like a real
> place instead of a long hallway.

Why it works: it starts with what I wanted, says what got dropped and why, then names the
thing it was traded for. The reasons are specific to the work, not to how impressive it
sounds. It ends on a change and what that change did, not on a lesson.

## More of the same voice

Cave Diver, 05 Where it's at:

> It's far from finished. Some rocks sit in odd places. The third dive needs work: it was
> supposed to be much harder, with tricks everywhere, but I didn't have time to build all of
> them. New players also need better help to understand how to play.

Seamless, hero:

> I shoot wide landscape photos, but phone screens are tall — the layout is portrait, not
> landscape. So a wide photo either sits small in the middle, or fills the frame and loses its
> sides. Seamless cuts one panorama into carousel slides: swipe through them and you see the
> whole picture.

## Rules that follow from it

- Open with what I wanted or what happened, not with a definition of the product.
- One claim per sentence. Short sentences are fine; a long one is fine when it carries a real
  chain of cause and effect.
- Use the specific thing: a number, a name, a piece of the interface, what actually happened.
- Say what got dropped and why. Say what is still missing. Do not balance it with a hopeful
  closer.
- First person for anything about the making. Second person for anything the reader does.
- Ordinary words: landscape, portrait, layout, carousel, pan, blur, export are all fine.
  No need to talk down to the reader.
- No "not X but Y" contrasts, no one-line closers, no forced triads, no sayings that sound
  deep, no sales words (nestled, breathtaking, testament, delve, seamless as an adjective).
- Em dashes are allowed, but keep them to about the rate of the samples above.
- Headings name what the section holds. No emoji, no decorative arrows in headings.

## What is not a reference

Vibecab and Skyeye are marked Work in progress. Their copy is a sketch and changes as the
projects change. Do not treat it as the voice.
